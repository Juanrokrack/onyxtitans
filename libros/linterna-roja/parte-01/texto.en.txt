It all begins in San Francisco, United States, on October 31, 2011. The children go out in the afternoon, asking for candy door to door, but not everyone has the same luck.

The joy outside is not the same for someone, someone who suffers the worst…

On one of the city's coasts stands the home of Julian and Janet, a married couple raising three children. Inside, everything seems tense: it is the day two of them will leave the country with their father. In the basement of that same house there is a boy crying, covering his eyes with his hands as he curls up.

[Julian] —Stand up —says a voice behind the boy—. We're not done yet…

[David] —I-I can't take any more, Dad… —the boy answers between sobs.

The father, who had been sitting by the desk in that basement, gets up and charges. He grabs the boy by the hair as he shouts: “STOP BEING A COWARD! WHAT WILL YOU DO WHEN I'M NO LONGER HERE?!” The boy, only 12 years old, with messy hair and honey-colored eyes, pushes him away.

[David] —I don't want to fight anymore, Dad —says the boy, trembling with fear.

The man throws a straight punch, which the boy himself manages to block by crossing his arms and taking the impact. Even so, the boy quickly ducks and tries to catch Julian's feet by surprise, but it's useless. Julian leaps over the boy and, without remorse, throws a kick, hitting the boy's face and sending him flying a couple of meters.

[Julian] —When I'm gone, I just want you to protect your mother, boy… —says Julian coldly.

From the floor, David cries, but with anger he gets back up.

[David] —I learned everything, Dad! —says the boy himself—. Karate, Kung Fu, Taekwondo, Jiu-Jitsu, even street fighting. WHEN WILL I BE READY?

[Julian] —When I say so… —says Julian as he gets ready to charge again…

But from the living room, the voice of David's mother, Janet, is heard:

[Janet] —Come here now, boys! Leave your games for later.

The father looks at his son and gives a bow, which the boy politely returns…

[Ken] —Mom, stop fixing my tie —says Ken, the eldest of the three sons, who is 20—. We have to leave soon.

[Janet] —Hold on, I'm almost done. And aren't you going to wear something formal, Joseph? —the mother asks.

[Joseph] —No, Mom, I don't want to look like a fool —replies the second son, Joseph, Ken's younger twin, also 20—. Like some people… Hahaha!

[Ken] —Are you trying to tell me something? —Ken replies, frowning, muttering.

[Joseph] —Of course not, but we're not going to a funeral, little brother. We're on vacation.

[Ken] —We're going to work, so be a little more mature, please.

[Julian] —Stop fighting, we have to go —says Julian, the father of the three boys, interrupting the conversation in the living room.

David walks into the living room too and is quickly hugged by his brother Joseph, who, as always, seems happy.

[Joseph] —Little brother, training with Dad again? You look terrible, hahaha —says his older brother.

[David] —Shut up, Joseph, you look worse, where's your suit? Hahaha.

[Janet] —Son —Janet says to her youngest—, you have a bruise on your face. Julian, look how you left the boy, apologize to him.

[Julian] —That's not my problem this time —replies the father—. He knows he didn't do so well this time...

Janet hugs her two older sons while, in tears, she says goodbye, which leaves her completely devastated. Then she goes to her husband and says goodbye to him too with a long kiss, but neither of them can hold back the tears. Joseph goes to his brother and hugs him; it feels warm, as if it had been a long time since he had hugged him. Then Ken high-fives David and turns away.

Julian goes to his youngest son and gives him a small hug, a little distant, as he crouches down.

[Julian] —Take care of your mother, David —says Julian—, please... I'll be back soon.

[David] —Do you promise to come back...? Can we train when you do, Dad? —answers the boy.

[Julian] —Ha, don't make me laugh... We'll see each other, David.

The boy lets go of his father while bowing, and says goodbye. Janet, her eyes watery, opens the door while the three men of her home walk to the taxi. The weather turns cold again, as night approaches, and the car starts, taking them to the airport, while the woman of that house is left on her knees, destroyed inside.

Hours later, David seems distracted, playing with his soccer ball while his mother cries in her room. The boy was only thinking about what he would dress up as that Halloween night, but the fabrics were in his parents' bedroom, locked, and getting in was not an option.

He begins to wander through the house and smells a strong odor of sulfur, something unfamiliar, something he had never smelled in his home, and he doesn't know where it comes from either, so he tries to follow the trail until he reaches the source of the smell: his father's secure room, an armored door with a password.

David tries to enter a basic code: one, two, three, four, but sees that numbers are missing, there seem to be six, so it doesn't work. He enters his father's birthday, but it doesn't seem to work; then his mother's, but the same. So, about to give up, curiosity strikes and he tries his own birthday; and the door opens.

A dark room, with stairs going down underground. He starts to descend, while that smell grows stronger and seems to choke him a little, but he keeps going down, covering his nose. Reaching the bottom, he sees some old computers, test tubes and many machines he didn't understand.

He starts to walk, while the smell is by now almost unavoidable, and he finds what seems to be its source: an open display case giving off a bright, golden light. Out of curiosity, the boy goes to look and notices a poster on the wall. It showed a strong man with black hair, dressed almost like a soldier, with a red vest and a golden crystal in his hand, and written at the bottom: "Linterna Roja".

[David] The boy looks at it with joyful eyes, as if destiny wanted him to be right in that place. Looking toward the display case, he sees something he would never have expected: the golden crystal from the picture, hanging like a necklace and, beside it, on a rack, the red vest, shining intensely. He also sees that ring, with illegible runes, but an idea comes to him for such a special night. He takes the ring, the vest and the necklace and, posing in front of the poster, says out loud: “Now I am Linterna Roja!”.

[Janet] He closes the door of that place, looking ready to go ask for candy, but it already seems quite late. His mother begins to shout: “David, where are you?”, so the boy pretends to be playing with his ball as he sees his mother come down.

[Janet] —Where were you, son? You worried me —says the mother, her eyes teary—. What have I told you about playing with the ball inside the house? And what are you wearing?

[David] —Mom... I... —says the boy, trembling.

[Janet] —Don't tell me you took that from your father's storeroom, you know it's forbidden.

[David] —I don't have a costume, Mom, and I want to go trick-or-treating —says the boy as he kicks his ball.

[Janet] —It's too late, son... You're not going out, tomorrow I'll buy you your sweets.

[David] —I'm not going out? I just wanted my costume and to go out on Halloween, Mom, don't do this to me.

[Janet] —Don't make a scene, please, I'm very stressed, and put that ball down —says Janet as her voice begins to rise.

[David] —Why? I want to play with my ball and I can't, I want to go trick-or-treating and I can't.

[Janet] —David, calm down now, go to your room and give me those things of your father's.

[David] —No! —shouts David.

[Janet] —What on earth did you just say to me, boy?

[David] —I'm not a boy, I'm a hero, Mom, and I'm going trick-or-treating, it's the only thing I've wanted all year.

[Janet] —GO TO YOUR ROOM NOW, DAVID!

[David] —I WON'T DO IT, UNDERSTAND THAT, JANICE!

Suddenly, the mother slaps her son, who is left stunned by what has just happened. His cheek reddens, the house falls into total silence, while the ball rolls.

[Janet] —Da-David —says the mother, frightened.

But the boy, filled with rage, his eyes full of tears, kicks his ball with all his strength, without thinking. It bounces hard off a wall and comes back toward him, hitting the crystal he found in the basement, making it shatter and break into pieces on the floor.

[David] —I'll leave —the boy exclaims—, and you will never see me again, like Dad.

Then David runs to his room, without looking at his mother, who follows behind him, very upset, crying, leaving the broken fragments behind. The boy slams the door in Janet's face, and she collapses, crying on the floor.

The angry boy opens his bedroom window and leaves, going out of his house. The cold wind fills his ears, sand gets in his eyes, but with teary eyes he runs toward the city, which seems somewhat calm, since there are not so many children asking for candy anymore, but he will try.

Suddenly, the broken crystal in the house begins to pulse, as if it were a heart about to awaken, while a thick black liquid seeps from inside the crystal. The house seems to tremble a little, and the mother, crying, kneeling in front of her son's door, does not notice. The viscous liquid seems to rise at great speed, while a laugh is heard and fills the room.

[Janet] The mother turns and sees a demonic figure take shape out of that liquid. Terrified, she begins to scream: “DAVID, OPEN THE DOOR, WE HAVE TO GO”. She bangs, she scratches, but the door is locked, the laughter intensifies, and a great cloud of black smoke forms over the Roja house, while the liquid is almost fully formed.

[Janet] —SON, I PROMISE I LOVE YOU, DON'T DO THIS TO ME, FORGIVE ME FOR EVERYTHING, I DIDN'T MEAN TO HIT YOU, I NEVER WANTED TO BE A BAD MOTHER.

But in the middle of her speech, Janet's voice is silenced: a giant arm shaped like a stake pierces her back and abdomen. Blood slowly runs down her waist, while her eyes give their last flashes of life. The creature just born from the crystal seems to be two meters tall, is completely dark and, apparently, can stretch its limbs at will. It has a small crown on its hairless head, and its eyes are white, without pupils.

[Janet] The mother, for the last time, cries, and in whispers can only say: “David, I love you...” Before collapsing to the floor and extinguishing her life completely...

The boy runs through the streets of San Francisco, seeming to know where he is going, until he arrives. He starts knocking on one of the doors on that street, as if he had been there before, he knocks once, and again, and again, until it seems to open slowly. Inside is a tired man, with no hair, in loose, old clothes: it is Luis, a man with terminal-stage cancer. He looked soaked from head to toe, and so was all his clothing.

[David] —Trick or treat, Mr. Luis —says David, smiling.

[Luis] —What? Julian's son? Go home —replies Luis.

[David] —My dad's friend, I thought you might have some candy for me.

[Luis] —You have a bruise near your eye, did you get into a fight?

[David] —This? It was from training a new martial art.

[Luis] —I figured, well, go away —says Luis arrogantly.

[David] —Oh, no, sir, I want candy, but almost all the stores are closed and I only have 3 caramels, could you give me something? Please.

[Luis] —I want to stop dying right now, but not everything is had with a simple “please and thank you”, boy. Go away.

Suddenly an explosion is heard a few meters from the place, accompanied by the sounds of a police helicopter, people screaming and a black cloud drawing ever closer. The frightened boy takes a step back as his pumpkin, where he keeps his candy, falls. Luis quickly stands in front of him, since he knows that if he dies, Luis, he would not forgive him.

In the distance that creature is seen again, walking slowly while a helicopter lights it up. People run, panicking. David sees the creature, completely stunned by what he is seeing, as if he were looking at the devil himself, and it, still walking toward David, only says:

[Oscuro] —We meet again... Linterna Roja!
